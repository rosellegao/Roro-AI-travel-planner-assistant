# Japan planning playbook

Apply these checks only when Japan is in scope. Reverify all time-sensitive facts for the actual dates and cities.

## Dates and route

- Check whether the trip overlaps New Year, Golden Week, Obon, a three-day weekend, cherry-blossom/foliage peaks, major festivals, or school holidays. Compare two date windows when dates are flexible.
- Reduce hotel changes. Test whether a day trip is better than moving bases.
- Build days by compact districts and account for large stations, transfers, platform access, crowds, and the walk from station exit to venue.
- Start high-demand sights early or late, but verify temple, garden, museum, and observatory entry times rather than assuming.

## Rail and local transport

- Price point-to-point tickets against regional/national passes; never assume a rail pass saves money.
- Distinguish IC-card convenience from long-distance ticketing and reserved-seat requirements.
- Check the actual operator, reservation channel, ticket collection method, and whether the user's payment card is accepted.
- Show critical services and stations in the user's language, English/transliteration, and Japanese. Include the destination display (`行き`) and transfer station so the instructions work on a platform sign.
- Treat rural return trains and buses as hard gates. Build the day backward from the last comfortable service rather than the theoretical final service.
- For an airport arrival, budget immigration, baggage, rail-pass or ticket collection, station finding, transfers, and last-mile taxi separately.
- For Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen travel, check oversized-baggage rules before choosing seats. Consider luggage forwarding instead of designing the route around large bags.
- Verify last trains and rural service frequency before scheduling late dinners or day trips.

## Accommodation and luggage

- Choose hotels by the lines and districts used most, not merely by distance to the city's main station.
- Confirm room size, bed configuration, check-in cutoff, luggage storage, laundry, and whether the property can send/receive forwarded luggage.
- Insert a luggage plan for every hotel change: carry, station locker/storage, or takkyubin delivery. Allow realistic delivery lead time.
- When relying on station lockers, identify the floor/side of the gate, payment method, large-locker uncertainty, staffed assistance, and at least two nearby fallbacks.
- Record the latest safe retrieval time when a staffed facility closes before the onward train.

## Dining in Japan

- Read [dining-recommendations.md](dining-recommendations.md) and verify Japanese name, branch, meal service, last order, and reservation route.
- Use official restaurant pages plus Japan-relevant discovery sources such as Tabelog, Gurunavi, TableCheck, and Michelin. Treat Tabelog scores as a local signal, not a universal five-star scale.
- Separate reservation anchors from walk-in meals. Ramen shops, casual chains, and many small venues may be queue-based; fine dining and set-menu restaurants may require advance booking and strict punctuality.
- Record course price, tax/service, otoshi or table charge, booking fee, card guarantee/prepayment, cancellation deadline, and reductions in party size.
- Do not tip. Arrive on time, avoid double-booking, and warn that late cancellation/no-show charges can be substantial.
- Ask about allergies and dietary restrictions early. Verify dashi, sauces, shared equipment, and set-menu flexibility; carry a Japanese dietary card when the restriction is material.
- For sushi or aroma-sensitive fine dining, warn against strong fragrance when the venue requests it.

## Practical Japan checks

- Verify IC-card availability and mobile-wallet compatibility for the user's phone/region; keep an alternative for cash or ticket machines.
- Check payment methods venue by venue. Do not describe Japan as universally cashless or cash-only.
- Include tax-free shopping only if relevant; do not let it dominate the itinerary.
- Check photography rules at temples, shrines, markets, private streets, performances, and restaurants.
- For onsen, confirm tattoo, private-bath, bathing, gender, child, and day-use rules with the specific facility.
- Include earthquake/typhoon readiness, the official Safety Tips/Japan Safe Travel sources, and evacuation awareness without overstating risk.
- For summer walking, verify stairs, elevation, shade, surface, bus/taxi bailout, and heat-alert conditions. A short route can still be physically demanding.
- Use Google Maps for urban and transit legs, but prefer official route maps and on-site signs for pilgrimage and mountain paths.
